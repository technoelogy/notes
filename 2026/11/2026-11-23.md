# 2026-11-23

## What I learned today
I posted a question in the AIS+ community: AaaS, AI agency or RaaS for a regulated niche. Four detailed replies came back.

- The common direction was AaaS first, with the receptionist as the product. Compliance is mostly a fixed cost, so building it once as a product beats redoing it for every client.
- RaaS (pay per result) looks risky in healthcare. The result usually lives in the client's system, so measuring it means access to patient data and monthly attribution arguments. Fees tied to patient volume can also raise legal questions in the US. I need a lawyer to check what applies in India.
- Pricing shape that came up more than once: setup fee plus monthly retainer, with a guarantee on what I control (response time, uptime, human handoffs), not on bookings.
- Start narrow: one practice type, one scheduling system, urgent or unclear cases go straight to a human, no medical advice from the agent.
- Log response time, handoffs and bookings from day one, so the monthly report writes itself.
- Every practice will ask "why this and not the software I already pay for." My answer is depth in one sensitive health category, where the flow and tone are shaped for it.

## What I'm changing
- Dropping free pilots. Charging a setup fee instead.
- Pricing the marketing tools separately from the receptionist, so the receptionist never reads as custom work.
